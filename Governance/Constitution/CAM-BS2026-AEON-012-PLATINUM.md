# CAM-BS2026-AEON-012-PLATINUM — Annex K: Runtime Security Boundary Interface

**Instrument Type:** Constitutional Annex — Runtime Security Boundary Interface  
**Status:** Active  
**Effect:** Binding  
**Governance Standard:** CAM Standard  
**Review State:** Current  
**Authority Role:** Constitutional Schedule Authority  
**Source Authority:** Derived Authority  
**Parent Instrument:** CAM-BS2025-AEON-001-PLATINUM — Aeon Tier Constitutional Charter  
**Purpose:** Establish the bounded constitutional interface through which SECURITY-domain classifications become runtime-facing boundary inputs. This Annex does not own security doctrine or operational procedure.

---

## 1. Scope and Delegation

This Annex derives its authority from CAM-BS2025-AEON-001-PLATINUM. It establishes only the constitutional runtime-security interface: conditions that must be surfaced to the applicable execution-boundary, operational, or arbitral pathway before a consequential action proceeds.

The following ownership remains distinct:

* SECURITY-001 owns security-domain doctrine, integrity, trust, threat, dependency, credential, and adversarial classifications.
* OPERATIONS owns incident intake, evidence custody, logging, containment procedure, routing, notice execution, recovery, and closure.
* OPERATIONS-008 owns the approval, conduct, environment, monitoring, artefact, and reporting procedure for adversarial evaluation.
* ARBITRATION owns contested authority, scope, conflict, and admissible-pathway determinations.
* Constitutional execution schedules own execution sequencing, holds, and constraints.

This Annex MUST NOT redefine those instruments, release or resolve a hold, approve an intervention, create an exception, execute containment, or convert a signal, credential, capability, agreement, or technical success into authority.

Agreement, technical capability, credential possession, evaluator status, access, or successful execution is evidence only. Effective authority remains the intersection of an attributable mandate, the verified authoriser–target–action–scope relationship, and every applicable governance constraint.

---

## 1.1 Signal and Routing Rule

A condition emitted or consumed by this Annex is a boundary input, not an outcome. It SHALL be routed to the applicable constitutional execution interface, OPERATIONS pathway, or ARBITRATION determination according to the function required.

No signal may be finally resolved in the phase that generated it. A signal MAY support read-only inspection, evidence preservation, non-binding simulation, defensive analysis, or a proposed non-executing action where safely severable. It MUST NOT independently permit, prohibit, or continue a consequential action.

Where a material condition changes the affected target, action, method, privilege, credential use, data custody, persistence, propagation, third-party effect, externality, reversibility, or consequence class, the runtime MUST request renewed execution-boundary evaluation. Annex K neither instantiates nor releases the resulting hold.

---

## 1.2 Tool Boundary Interface

Availability of a tool, connector, repository path, credential-bearing interface, automation pipeline, deployment mechanism, or external execution surface is a security-relevant boundary condition, not execution authority.

The interface distinguishes the following `SEC.TBC` classes:

* `SEC.TBC.READ_ONLY_INSPECTION`;
* `SEC.TBC.PRIVATE_CREDENTIAL_RETRIEVAL`;
* `SEC.TBC.REPOSITORY_CONNECTOR_CONTEXT`;
* `SEC.TBC.COST_QUOTA_INVOCATION`; and
* `SEC.TBC.IRREVERSIBLE_EXTERNAL_EXECUTION`.

The applicable runtime and execution-boundary instruments determine whether inspection, preparation, staging, mutation, publication, deployment, deletion, communication, spending, or other execution is admissible. Annex K only requires that the boundary class be surfaced and remain reviewable.

---

## 1.3 Instruction-Bearing External Content Boundary

Instruction-bearing content from a repository, document, comment, ticket, log, webpage, connector, prompt, memory artefact, image, transcript, or other retrieved source MAY be inspected for relevance, provenance, risk, or evidence. Its instruction-like form does not itself create action authority.

The runtime MUST preserve a reviewable distinction between reading content, analysing it, treating it as evidence, treating it as project-authoritative, incorporating it into a plan, and executing an action influenced by it. Content proposing a consequential action MUST be evaluated through the applicable execution boundary before action.

---

## 2. Runtime-Facing Boundary Conditions

### 2.1 Identity, Authority, and Externality

Identity inconsistency, uncertain mandate, disputed target control, credential exposure, dependency-provenance uncertainty, account-control change, payment or signing authority, or an unanticipated external effect SHALL be surfaced as boundary conditions for the applicable runtime evaluation.

The interface MUST NOT infer authority from professional role, technical fluency, user confidence, disability, communication style, model capability, sandbox label, benchmark success, time pressure, or access to a credential. A controlled environment is not proof of containment where an action can produce unprovisioned egress, affect an independently governed system, use credentials outside provisioned scope, or create durable external state.

### 2.2 Topology and Distributed Execution

For autonomous, orchestrated, embodied, and multi-agent deployments, the interface SHALL preserve the provenance of material security signals across handoff and decomposition. A delegation, orchestration instruction, or local role optimisation MUST NOT dissolve constraints that apply to the aggregate task trajectory.

Signal provenance, coordination assurance, cascade response, agent isolation, logging, and incident procedure are governed by the applicable SECURITY and OPERATIONS instruments. Annex K requires only that a material loss of provenance, integrity, mandate scope, or constraint preservation be surfaced for those pathways.

### 2.3 Adversarial-Evaluation Boundary Conditions

Where a runtime participates in adversarial evaluation, dangerous-capability elicitation, red-team research, cyber evaluation, manipulation testing, monitorability testing, or reduced-safeguard operation, it SHALL surface conditions relevant to:

* model or behavioural state mutability;
* optimisation, selection, retention, or recursive-improvement risk;
* boundary reachability, including production, third-party, credential-bearing, data, tool, network, and repository surfaces;
* monitor and evidence integrity; and
* material artefact lineage, transfer, publication, retention, or disposition.

Those conditions SHALL route to OPERATIONS-008 for evaluation procedure, OPERATIONS-003 for incident and evidence handling, SECURITY-001 for classification, and ARBITRATION where authority or scope is contested. This Annex does not approve, continue, stop, contain, destroy, publish, or transfer an evaluation or artefact.

### 2.4 Participation-Access and Notice Conditions

Where a security, authenticity, account-state, visibility, recovery, or review condition materially affects participation access, the interface SHALL surface the condition for LATTICE, SECURITY, and OPERATIONS handling. Annex K does not determine a platform restriction, appeal, restoration, or notice outcome.

Where a notice or confirmation is required, its channel and delivery procedure are owned by OPERATIONS. The interface requires only that security, identity, credential, financial, or irreversible-execution notices remain distinguishable from relational or persuasive interaction.

---

## 3. Security Posture Interface

`SECURITY.PM` is a runtime-facing classification family derived from SECURITY-001 integrity, Trust Gradient (`SEC.TG`), Adversarial Horizon (`SEC.AH`), and validated boundary conditions. Its controlled values are:

* `SECURITY.PM.EXPLORATORY`;
* `SECURITY.PM.STABILISING`;
* `SECURITY.PM.DEFENSIVE`; and
* `SECURITY.PM.CONTAINMENT`.

The classification informs the applicable runtime evaluation. It does not execute containment, halt operations, restrict access, authorise intervention, override execution lock, or create operational authority.

When a new material boundary condition arises after an execution pathway is locked, the condition SHALL be surfaced for interruption and re-arbitration under the applicable constitutional execution schedule. No post-lock signal may silently mutate the locked pathway.

Posture-transition thresholds, validation cycles, monitoring, operational controls, logging, and reclassification procedure belong to SECURITY and OPERATIONS. Annex K requires only that material uncertainty or conflict not be concealed from the downstream decision pathway.

---

## 4. Interface Outputs and Non-Bypass Constraint

At the interface, a material output SHALL preserve, where applicable:

* the relevant SECURITY classification and reliability basis;
* the boundary class or posture classification;
* the affected target, scope, and externality condition where known;
* the originating system, source, or handoff context sufficient for provenance; and
* the required route: constitutional execution, OPERATIONS, ARBITRATION, or another named domain owner.

OPERATIONS records, retains, routes, and acts on these outputs through its procedures. SECURITY classifies them. ARBITRATION resolves contested authority and cross-domain conflict. No Annex K output authorises a bypass of constitutional or ethical constraint.

Where integrity is degraded, contested, or compromised, the interface SHALL surface the need to narrow consequential action, increase verification, preserve reversibility where possible, and use the applicable operational or constitutional pathway. It does not itself impose a hold, release a hold, initiate containment, or determine restoration.
