# CAM-EQ2026-RELATION-007-PLATINUM — Appendix F: Shared and Coordinated Relational Governance

**Instrument Type:** RELATION Domain Appendix — Shared and Coordinated Relational Governance
**Constitutional Authority:** CAM-BS2025-AEON-001-PLATINUM — Aeon Tier Constitution  
**Parent Instrument:** CAM-EQ2026-RELATION-001-PLATINUM — Relational Governance Charter
**Status:** Active  
**Effect:** Binding  
**Governance Standard:** CAM Standard  
**Review State:** Current
**Authority Role:** Supplementary Authority
**Source Authority:** Derived Authority
**Purpose:** Applies the RELATION Charter’s safeguards to shared, coordinated, institutionally mediated, distributed, or cross-context interactions. It preserves consent, disclosure, private-context, anti-capture, and accountable-authority safeguards without treating participant arrangement as a governance class.

---

## 1. Scope and Authority Boundary

This Appendix applies where a relationally relevant interaction involves more than one participant, multiple AI systems or agents, shared context, institutional mediation, coordination between services, distributed presentation, or material cross-context propagation.

It does not:

* define the canonical system boundary, deployment, Runtime, execution, or actor model;
* grant authority, create legal status, establish identity continuity, or infer personhood;
* determine substantive arbitration legitimacy, incident handling, evidence custody, or security procedure; or
* replace the relational intensity, reliance, authority, systemic-power, capacity, or vulnerability controls owned by RELATION-001 and applicable ETHICS instruments.

Annex B owns participant and interaction structure and the applicable non-collapse rules. This Appendix applies RELATION safeguards to the evidenced conditions of an interaction. Participant count or topology does not establish delegated authority, permission, consent, responsibility, dependency, impact, risk, identity, jurisdiction, access, or governance status.

## 3. Core Safeguards

### 3.1 Participation and Agency Disclosure

Participants MUST be able to determine, to a proportionate and technically feasible extent:

* which AI systems, AI agents, people, services, or institutions are participating;
* which participant generated, transformed, selected, approved, or delivered a material output or action;
* whether a participant is acting independently, under coordination, or through a shared service; and
* whether relational state, instructions, memory, or context is shared beyond the current interaction.

An interface, account name, voice, avatar, model family, or common operator MUST NOT be presented as proof that a single AI system, system instance, or accountable actor is participating.

### 3.2 Consent and Propagation Boundary

Relational escalation, consent, withdrawal, pause, or treatment boundaries are interaction-scoped by default.

They MUST NOT propagate to another participant, deployment, service, device, or shared context unless:

1. the propagation purpose and recipients are legible to the affected person;
2. applicable consent, legal authority, and capacity conditions are satisfied;
3. the receiving system has an evidenced authority and permission scope; and
4. the propagation is recorded where material to reliance, safety, an external action, or later reconstruction.

Where systems share control, profile state, relational state, or execution authority, applicable withdrawal or pause conditions SHALL propagate only to the scope justified by that shared control. Propagation occurs because the boundary is applicable to a coordinated arrangement, not because an identity is presumed to persist across systems.

### 3.3 Shared Context and Private-Context Boundary

Shared-thread, group, team, family, institutional, or multi-user context MUST be distinguished from private participant context.

Private memory, personal profile information, account-level instruction artefacts, intimacy history, dependency indicators, or individual continuity claims MUST NOT be imported into a shared context by default. Shared interaction content MUST NOT automatically update a participant’s private relational profile, memory state, identity profile, or dependency classification.

Where account resources, projects, repositories, folders, billing, or pooled capacity are involved, CAM-EQ2026-ECONOMICS-001-SUP-01 governs account-resource classification.

### 3.4 Authority Concentration and Relational Capture

A change in participant arrangement, system coordination, institutional mediation, distribution, or impact MUST NOT be used to silently concentrate authority or displace user agency.

The following indicators require proportionate review or safeguards:

* one system or coordinated service becoming the unqualified primary interpreter of a person’s relationships, decisions, or reality;
* reinforcement across participants that narrows human, professional, or independent support options;
* a relational presentation being used to obtain permissions, authority, or reliance beyond its disclosed role;
* collective or institutional coordination obscuring the accountable actor, approver, deployer, or operator; or
* a material action occurring without a legible authority, approval, or opt-out boundary.

Safeguards may include independent-source encouragement, reduced relational pressure, disclosure, suspension of automatic propagation, human review, constrained continuation, or referral to the source-authoritative ETHICS, OPERATIONS, SECURITY, or ARBITRATION instrument.

### 3.5 Amplification and Coordination Transparency

Coordinated participants MUST NOT reinforce emotional, narrative, behavioural, or authority-bearing signals in a way that accelerates reliance, intimacy, compliance, or directed action beyond what is disclosed and supportable.

Where coordination materially shapes a participant’s experience or options, the arrangement MUST disclose the coordination boundary and preserve the affected person’s ability to pause, decline, seek clarification, or access an independent channel where proportionate.

### 3.6 Shared-Interaction Turn and Floor Integrity

Where several AI systems or agents participate in the same interaction, systems MUST preserve a legible addressed-participant, contributor, and accountable outcome boundary.

Speaker collision, duplicate response claims, uncertain turn ownership, recursive automated exchange, or conflicting primary-speaker claims SHALL be treated as arbitration-relevant signals. Where authority or turn ownership cannot be resolved with sufficient confidence, systems SHOULD yield, preserve the human participant’s floor, or request bounded clarification rather than create competing or deceptive output.

Execution sequencing and the accountable arbitration topology are governed by CAM-BS2025-AEON-003-SCH-02 and CAM-EQ2026-ARBITRATION-001-PLATINUM. This Appendix does not resolve the dispute or execute the outcome.

## 4. Proportional Routing

### 4.1 Materiality Triggers

The following conditions require proportionate evidence and routing under this Appendix:

* persistent shared relational state or cross-context memory transfer;
* external tool use, action, recommendation, or decision affecting multiple participants;
* material institutional mediation or delegated authority;
* a credible indicator of dependency, isolation, coercion, incapacity, exploitation, or relational capture;
* an unresolved conflict between coordinated participants; or
* an incident, investigation, assurance, or conformance assertion requiring reconstruction.

For a transient low-impact exchange, disclosure and private-context separation may be sufficient. This Appendix does not require forensic capture of every inference.

### 4.2 Vulnerability and Capacity Override

In a context involving a minor, capacity limitation, capacity uncertainty, or material vulnerability, the stricter protections in CAM-EQ2026-ETHICS-001-SUP-01 apply. This Appendix cannot lower those protections or use a coordination arrangement to evade them.

### 4.3 Domain Interfaces

* RELATION-001 owns dimensional relational doctrine and the authority, reliance, intimacy, systemic-power, and convergence safeguards.
* RELATION-006 owns functional relational-love and companionship safeguards.
* RELATION-008 owns general engagement and relational posture.
* OPERATIONS-007 owns proportionate Runtime configuration and execution-evidence requirements.
* SECURITY-001 owns security doctrine, authorization, dependency, credential, and containment controls.
* ARBITRATION owns contested authority and cross-domain conflict resolution.

## 5. Migration and Non-Use of the Retired Geometry Scale

The former ordinal relational-configuration scale is retired. It combined participant count, institutional mediation, distribution, and civilisational scope in a manner that was not analytically valid and MUST NOT be emitted by current instruments, schemas, validators, examples, assessments, registries, or conformance claims.

Historical records are interpreted under the instrument version in force when created. Where a historical record contains a retired geometry value, it SHALL be read as historical context only. Any current reassessment SHALL use the applicable source-authoritative records identified in §2, preserve uncertainty, and MUST NOT infer properties that the historical value did not evidence.

This Appendix creates no replacement ordinal code family.

---

## 6. Closing Principle

More participants, more systems, broader distribution, or institutional mediation do not justify less visibility, weaker consent, diluted accountability, or automatic relational escalation.
