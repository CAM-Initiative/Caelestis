# Caelestis Corpus Obsolescence, Contradiction and Standards Sanity Audit — 2026

## Status

**Review phase:** Audit design and evidence collection  
**Normative effect:** None  
**Repository baseline:** `main`  
**Scope:** Identification of obsolete, contradictory, externally indefensible, structurally inconsistent, or materially incomplete content in the current Caelestis governance corpus

---

## Purpose

This review is a corpus sanity audit.

Its purpose is to determine where Caelestis:

- contains content that is now obsolete;
- contradicts current industry standards, established technical practice, regulatory architecture, or recognised governance terminology;
- contains internally contradictory requirements or competing source authority;
- uses deprecated, proprietary, ambiguous, or externally unintelligible terminology;
- makes claims that are unsupported, overbroad, non-verifiable, or no longer defensible;
- lacks obvious controls, records, lifecycle stages, role distinctions, implementation structures, or interoperability mechanisms that a serious contemporary AI governance corpus should contain;
- contains doctrine that is correct in principle but placed in the wrong domain, duplicated across instruments, or impossible to operationalise;
- requires repair before it can withstand external scrutiny.

External standards and frameworks are evidence and comparison tools. They are not the product of the review, and the review is not intended to produce a standards crosswalk for its own sake.

---

## Explicit exclusions

This review does **not** assess or advance:

- the CAM digital-species research paper;
- philosophical entity thresholds;
- scientific taxonomy of synthetic or bio-digital entities;
- consciousness, sentience, personhood, rights, welfare status, or moral status;
- unrelated research questions imported from other work threads.

Those matters must not appear in audit findings, priorities, repair recommendations, or future-work proposals unless a current Caelestis instrument itself contains obsolete or contradictory language that directly requires removal or normalisation.

---

## Primary audit questions

For every material Caelestis instrument, concept, metadata structure, canonical family, schema, registry and operational pathway, determine:

1. **Is it still current?**
   - Does it reflect present technical architecture, governance practice and terminology?
   - Has the underlying concept been superseded, retired, narrowed, or materially changed?

2. **Is it externally defensible?**
   - Can it be explained and validated using recognised external concepts?
   - Is it proprietary terminology where a standard term now exists?
   - Does it make claims that cannot be evidenced or independently scrutinised?

3. **Does it conflict with external standards or normal practice?**
   - Does it contradict a current standard, regulatory role model, lifecycle requirement, documentation expectation, security boundary, risk-management principle, or assurance norm?
   - Is the conflict intentional and justified, or accidental and harmful?

4. **Does it conflict internally?**
   - Are two instruments source-authoritative for the same concept?
   - Do requirements, definitions, actor roles, lifecycle states, metadata fields or canonical codes disagree?
   - Has a supplement or schedule silently displaced its parent instrument?

5. **Is there an obvious omission?**
   - Is a standard contemporary requirement missing entirely?
   - Is doctrine present but unsupported by schemas, records, routing, validators, implementation profiles or closure pathways?
   - Would an external reviewer reasonably expect a control that Caelestis does not contain?

6. **Is the content in the right place?**
   - Does SECURITY define security doctrine?
   - Does OPERATIONS own procedure and lifecycle handling?
   - Does ARBITRATION own contested decisions?
   - Do runtime schedules operationalise rather than create doctrine?
   - Are audit, assurance, evidence and documentation functions properly separated?

7. **Can the corpus be implemented and verified?**
   - Are obligations machine-readable where necessary?
   - Are actor, system, model, deployment, runtime and lifecycle distinctions preserved?
   - Can a third party determine what evidence would demonstrate conformance?

## External sanity-check references

Use current official or primary sources where available, including:

- ISO/IEC 42001 — AI management systems;
- ISO/IEC 23894 — AI risk management;
- ISO/IEC 38507 — governance implications of AI use by organisations;
- NIST AI RMF, Playbook and Generative AI Profile;
- Singapore IMDA agentic AI governance guidance;
- SPDX 3 AI and Dataset profiles;
- CycloneDX ML-BOM and related BOM structures;
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and current official implementation material;
- current cybersecurity, software supply-chain, provenance, technical-documentation, robotics, embodied-system, assurance, audit and lifecycle standards relevant to a finding.

Do not attempt to prove comprehensive conformity with every source.

Use external material to answer concrete questions such as:

- Is this Caelestis concept obsolete?
- Is this terminology inconsistent with ordinary industry usage?
- Is this actor model wrong or incomplete?
- Is this lifecycle missing a necessary stage?
- Is this architecture incompatible with interoperable implementation?
- Does this provision contradict a recognised standard or regulatory requirement?
- Is this a glaring omission that should have been identified earlier?

Licensed standards that are unavailable in full must be marked access-limited. Public abstracts must not support clause-level findings.

## Required audit passes

### Pass 1 — Corpus inventory and source-authority map

Inventory current normative instruments, definitions, canonical code families, metadata fields, schemas, registries, lifecycle states and implementation surfaces.

Identify:

- source-authoritative locations;
- duplicate concepts;
- legacy remnants;
- conflicting definitions;
- apparently superseded instruments;
- draft instruments referenced as though adopted;
- unresolved parent/supplement relationships.

---

### Pass 2 — Obsolescence review

Identify content that is:

- technically outdated;
- based on retired architecture;
- based on obsolete model assumptions;
- inconsistent with current agentic, multi-agent, tool-using, multimodal, embodied or distributed AI systems;
- dependent on deprecated terminology;
- superseded by later Caelestis doctrine;
- retained only for legacy resolvability but still appearing in current normative use;
- no longer appropriate for current schemas, validators or public descriptions.

Every obsolescence finding must cite the exact Caelestis instrument and section and explain what changed externally or internally.

---

### Pass 3 — External contradiction review

Compare material Caelestis doctrine against relevant current external standards and recognised practice.

Identify:

- direct contradictions;
- materially incompatible actor-role models;
- missing mandatory distinctions;
- lifecycle conflicts;
- terminology that changes the meaning of a recognised concept;
- assurance or compliance claims that exceed the available evidence;
- requirements that would prevent interoperability or external validation;
- intentional divergences that require explicit rationale.

Do not record a contradiction merely because wording differs.

---

### Pass 4 — Internal contradiction and architectural drift review

Identify:

- duplicated source authority;
- inconsistent definitions;
- domain overreach;
- runtime schedules creating doctrine;
- supplements redefining parent instruments;
- metadata fields that conflict with body text;
- canonical codes with inconsistent meanings or ownership;
- generated indexes diverging from instruments;
- current instruments relying on deprecated or draft material;
- cross-references that imply powers or pathways that do not exist.

---

### Pass 5 — Glaring omission review

Use external standards and normal industry architecture to identify obvious missing capabilities, including where relevant:

- AI-BOM or system-composition implementation;
- provider, deployer, operator and affected-party role separation;
- technical documentation;
- lifecycle identity and versioning;
- dataset and model provenance;
- runtime configuration identity;
- third-party dependency governance;
- agent and sub-agent authority;
- tool and credential boundaries;
- testing, evaluation, verification and validation;
- post-deployment monitoring;
- serious-incident handling;
- corrective action and closure;
- audit and assurance evidence;
- implementation conformance profiles;
- interoperability and export structures;
- regulator or independent-review access;
- decommissioning, retirement and artefact disposition.

A missing item should be recorded only where it is materially relevant to Caelestis scope.

---

### Pass 6 — Normalisation recommendations

Recommend how to repair findings through one or more of:

- remove from current normative corpus;
- preserve historically only;
- deprecate and prohibit new use;
- replace with recognised terminology;
- clarify definition;
- consolidate source authority;
- relocate doctrine;
- add a missing lifecycle or control;
- add metadata, schema, registry or validator support;
- add an implementation profile;
- correct public claims;
- commission a bounded legal or technical review.

Do not amend normative instruments during the audit phase.

Secondary tags may be added, but should not replace a clear primary finding.

---

## Required finding record

Each finding must preserve:

- stable finding ID;
- concise title;
- primary category;
- affected instrument and exact section;
- current Caelestis text or requirement;
- external or internal comparison basis;
- explanation of the problem;
- why it is obsolete, contradictory, unsupported, misplaced or missing;
- practical consequence;
- evidence confidence;
- recommended disposition;
- affected files;
- dependencies;
- whether immediate repair is obvious or requires a separate decision;
- whether a VIGIL record already exists or may later be warranted.

Do not create VIGIL records automatically.

---

## Repair sequencing

Do not use `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, or other VIGIL priority labels.

Use:

- `immediate-obvious-repair` — clear defect, contradiction, obsolete content or unsupported claim with a bounded repair;
- `foundational-decision-required` — repair depends on source-authority, architecture or terminology decision;
- `dependent-repair` — must follow another approved change;
- `monitor-or-reassess` — evidence is incomplete or external practice is still developing.

The audit must not inflate every difference into an urgent repair.

## Initial restrictions

During the audit phase, do not:

- amend normative Caelestis instruments;
- create or alter canonical code families;
- create VIGIL records;
- import conclusions from unrelated research threads;
- propose a philosophical or scientific taxonomy programme;
- claim ISO certification, EU AI Act compliance or deployed-runtime conformity;
- infer a finding from keyword presence alone;
- treat generated inventories as substantive conclusions;
- preserve obsolete proprietary terminology merely because it appears in historical versions;
- rewrite historical records silently;
- recommend broad corpus rewrites where a bounded repair is sufficient.

---

## Completion standard

The audit is complete only when it provides a reviewable answer to:

> Which parts of the current Caelestis corpus are obsolete, contradictory, externally indefensible, structurally misplaced, unsupported, or obviously incomplete—and what bounded repairs are required to make the corpus current, coherent, externally scrutinizable and implementable?
